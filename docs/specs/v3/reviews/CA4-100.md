# CA4-100 — deadline construction from a duration

**Status:** specification reviewed (2026-10-14).
**Evidence type:** `textual_contract_and_scenario_review`.
**Runtime test:** `not_run_v3_not_implemented` (the counterexample was observed in the
implementation worktree with native Python 3.14.5 `timedelta`; that is not V3 runtime evidence).

## Impact record

- **Approved rules.** Every declared instant is a microsecond `EvidenceInstant` (Layer 0 §3,
  Layer 1 §4); `Window` and `IntervalRecurrence` accept any positive finite non-boolean number
  with no silent correction (Layer 0 §3.1–3.2); CA4-99 basis references.
- **Gap.** `anchor + duration` may be below, between or beyond microsecond instants:
  `Window(0.0000001)` became `due == now` and could loop; `0.0000015` s fell between instants; a
  large duration or horizon could overflow. Local rules already differed: Layer 2 source-age
  expiry (first instant strictly after), Layer 5 learned delays (whole milliseconds at staging).
- **Window audit.** Deadline producers: `Stability` dwell/grace, fixed-duration holds, Reaction
  delays and step intervals, `IntervalRecurrence`, confirmation ceiling, Layer-6 digest window,
  Layer-2 source-age expiry, Layer-3 signal expiry. Horizon ends: calendar upcoming reads.
  Non-deadline uses (buffer and history horizons, learning windows, `WarmupGate`, `LearnedWindow`
  bounds) only compare durations and are unaffected. Calibrated durations are produced at runtime,
  so a staging-only rejection could not cover them.

## Decision (approved, B + 1(a) + 2(a))

- `Window` unchanged; one consumer rule in Layer 0 §3.1 for every instant derived from an anchor
  plus a positive duration.
- The duration's value is the decimal of its canonical RFC-8785 form, summed exactly.
- The derived instant is the first `EvidenceInstant` at or after the exact sum: never before the
  requested duration, always after the anchor.
- Stricter consumers keep their rule (Layer 2 strictly after, Layer 5 whole milliseconds).
- Beyond `9999-12-31T23:59:59.999999+00:00`: dynamic registration rejected with a diagnostic;
  static horizon rejected at staging (this defines the calendar horizon limit).
- Execution lateness is distinct; CA4-98/99 unchanged.

## Alternatives rejected

- Whole-microsecond (or whole-millisecond) restriction on every `Window`: changes the shared
  validator, needs an arbitrary maximum, and still has to refuse or round runtime-calibrated values.
- Consumer-side rejection of non-integer microseconds: a calibrated duration would drop its job and
  stop a recurrence.
- Binary-value interpretation: `0.1` s would become 100 001 µs.

## Scenario review

| Duration from `2026-10-14T12:00:00.000000+00:00` | Due |
|---|---|
| `0.0000001` | `…:00.000001` (never the anchor; recurrence re-arms 1 µs later) |
| `0.000001` | `…:00.000001` |
| `0.0000015` | `…:00.000002` |
| `0.000002` | `…:00.000002` |
| `0.1` | `…:00.100000` exactly |
| Sum after `9999-12-31T23:59:59.999999+00:00` | Dynamic: rejected with diagnostic; static horizon: staging error |
| Timer fires 5 ms after due | Lateness only; due unchanged |
